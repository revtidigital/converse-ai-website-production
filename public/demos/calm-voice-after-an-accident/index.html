AndyYeah, that’s right.

Scenario 2: The renewal call, as recorded
CarolHello! This is Carol from Aurelia Insurance. This call may be recorded. I’m calling because your health policy renewal is coming up. Is this Emily?
EmilyI already know about the renewal. Why do you people keep calling? It’s annoying.
CarolI’m very sorry for the frustration. Since you’re already aware, I’ll keep this brief.
EmilyI don’t know if I want to renew. Your premium is too expensive. And I never got the AD&D rider price.
CarolYour current renewal premium is $612.40. Adding AD&D brings the total to $650.90, so the rider itself is $38.50.
EmilyWhat benefits do I get?
CarolThe rider pays a lump sum in the event of accidental death or permanent disability, adding a financial safety net.
EmilyIf I switch provider, will the waiting period start over?
CarolTypically, yes. By renewing with Aurelia, you keep the waiting-period progress you have already made.
EmilyLet’s stick to one year only for now.
CarolWe’ll stick with the one-year term. You can complete payment through the Aurelia app or website under Pay Now.
Common pushback

Questions People Ask, and Honest Answers

Question

"Can AI really handle a sensitive claim call?"

Answer: With the right boundaries, yes. In the recording, she checks on his safety first, stays warm about it, verifies the policy properly, and is clear that logging a claim isn't the same as approving one.

Question

"What if the policy lookup fails?"

Answer: It doesn't derail the call — she just falls back to asking for the last four digits of the policy number and confirms from there.

Question

"Can it handle someone who's already annoyed?"

Answer: That's literally how the renewal call starts. She acknowledges it, keeps things brief, answers the question that's actually been bothering Emily, and the call turns useful instead of adversarial.

Question

"Will it promise something it shouldn't?"

Answer: It explains policy details and next steps — it doesn't approve claims, settle anything, or make binding commitments on the insurer's behalf.

After the walkthrough

Questions Worth Asking Next

Use these right after the recordings to figure out where this would actually help first — claims, renewals, or both.

How claims run today
  • How many claim calls come in on a typical day?
  • What share of those are first-notice-of-loss calls?
  • What has to be captured before a claim can be logged?
  • How often does phone-based policy lookup just fail?
  • Which claim types would make sense to start with?
How renewals run today
  • What's the lapse rate by product line?
  • How many renewal reminders end up needing an actual call?
  • What are the most common objections you hear?
  • Which riders or add-ons confuse people the most?
  • What share of renewal calls actually end in payment?
Systems already in place
  • Where do policy, claim, and rider details live today?
  • Can your web form or campaign tool fire a webhook?
  • What system needs to receive claim updates?
  • What system needs to receive renewal outcomes?
  • What do you actually want to see on a dashboard?
Governance and compliance
  • What consent language is required for call recording?
  • What is the AI allowed to say about claims and coverage?
  • When does a call need to go to a licensed adjuster instead?
  • What does the audit trail need to include?
  • Any data retention or residency requirements to plan around?
The one question worth asking first

"Where's the biggest gap right now — how long claims take to log, how anxious customers get, how many renewals lapse, or how much manual follow-up your team is stuck doing?"
